# Review scope and limitations

Hashstash is experimental software. The maintained tests exercise accounting, execution and interfaces. This document describes their scope and the risks those checks do not resolve; it is not an independent audit report.

Accounting and consensus tests cover conservation in each outcome, deposits into an empty flat reserve, price preservation for protocol reserve movements, directed integer curve bounds, simultaneous amount-only clearing, proportional fills and refunds, per-block price bounds, grant eligibility and escrow, exact PASS/FAIL leading-block comparison, settlement, reorganization and overflow limits. Multi-recipient cases include bounded decoding, canonical recipient ordering, aggregate funding limits, prepaid execution weight, atomic credits and miner-account overlap. The [validation guide](VALIDATION.md) explains how to repeat the maintained checks and record their results.

The protocol has six direct operations, exact signed fees, whole-fee miner/revenue allocation, compact binary records and bounded binary peer transport. Browser and cold signing bind the chain ID, block reference and lifetime in the chain-bound transaction domain. The chain ID is signed but not repeated in the body, so a signature verifies only on its own network; wallet export network context is checked against the signer's selected network, and node ancestry validation establishes that the anchor belongs to the accepting branch. Tests cover the inclusive two-block boundary, altered references, reorganization invalidation, immutable retries and replay. These checks do not guarantee prompt inclusion or a quoted trade price. Application data and recipient-hidden Mail are separate from the reserve mechanics.

The flat baseline deliberately permits equal-price outcomes that resolve FAIL. Both outcomes open on the same curve, and revenue waits during a decision, so neither outcome's price is structurally easier to move. A grant draws at most half of the reserve, so that shared curve stays tradeable; FAIL trades during a reserve-drawing decision still execute on it, and when FAIL is selected they can leave value with waiting revenue or draw it into the reserve. Integer rounding on a nearly flat curve still lets buys raise either price slightly, while small sales may fail to lower it or even raise it. In auctions, a higher bid that is later cancelled hands the lead back with its original start, and the leader at the timeout wins. A bidder with enough liquidity can still delay another proposal's opening until the auction timeout, for example with a series of higher grants that sales later make unfundable, at the cost of fees. It cannot end the round without a decision: whoever leads at the timeout opens, and that bid becomes revenue. A flat curve moves only upward, by the sub-atom rounding remainders of buys and sales alike, and not at all at the genesis exchange rate. Correct accounting does not prove economic stability, representative participation, manipulation resistance or project value.

Authenticated peers prove possession of transport keys and protect message integrity. They cannot prevent all partitions, compromised peers, eclipse attacks or distributed resource attacks. Discovery can find peers, but a public key alone does not establish an independent or trustworthy operator. Account state, retained history and restart replay still grow.

Difficulty adjustment anchors each branch at its first mined blocks, so waiting before mining starts does not lower the initial difficulty. The anchor is bounded below by the second block's timestamp less one interval: backdating only the first block cannot convert the wait into ASERT error, although a miner of both first blocks still can. On a network created with `init --hashrate`, the target limit keeps blocks at most 256 times cheaper than the calibrated initial target. Pauses after mining begins do affect ASERT's elapsed time. Timestamp checks constrain the inputs but do not eliminate manipulation. Nodes must execute the same consensus rules as well as share the same genesis configuration.

Browser wallets trust the serving origin; stored keys are unencrypted. Public transaction actions and senders remain visible. Mail verifies the publishing transaction signature locally before decrypting contents; inclusion and confirmation counts still depend on the connected node. It does not hide the ledger, and static-key compromise can expose historical mail.

Tests and source review are not independent security certification. Dependency advisories need separate, repeatable review against the locked versions. Long-running adversarial networks, deployment security and real mining delivery require evidence beyond local tests.
